## The Pain-Point Solver
**Sample voice:** "I have really bad shoulder pain when I side sleep. Does this actually help with that, or is it just another 'best for everyone' mattress?"

- **Traffic share:** ~20-25% of quiz/site traffic
- **Behavior:** Arrives searching by a specific physical issue (back, hip, shoulder, or neck pain) or a defined sleep position, most commonly side sleeper. Mobile-first, evening browsing. Converts only when content clearly maps their exact pain point to a product.
- **Motivations:** Resolve a specific, named discomfort. Not shopping for a mattress in the abstract, shopping for relief from something concrete.
- **Trusts:** Specific claims tied to their exact issue (e.g. "engineered for side sleepers with hip pain"). Third-party validation of pain-relief claims.
- **Distrusts:** Generic "great for everyone" language. Pages that don't acknowledge their specific need within the first screen.
- **Demographics:** General — no segment-specific demographic signal in the data.
- **Data sources:** 3QQ quiz funnel (Side Sleeper 11.8K, Back Pain 9.5K, Hip/Shoulder/Neck Pain events); paid campaign data ("Side Sleepers - Desktop", "Heavy Sleepers - Mobile" targeting specific need).

## The Evening Researcher
**Sample voice:** "I don't want to just buy the first one that pops up — show me why this beat the others, not just that it did."

- **Traffic share:** 40%+ of volume (largest segment)
- **Behavior:** Browses roundup content on mobile in evenings/weekends, follows a roundup → review → comparison path, then converts later on desktop.
- **Motivations:** Wants to feel confident in the decision before buying — values expert opinion over brand loyalty, isn't rushing.
- **Trusts:** Expert-authored tone, third-party validation, clear editorial reasoning for rankings.
- **Distrusts:** Anything that feels like a hard sell or rushes them past the comparison step.
- **Demographics:** General. Loosely aligns with "Shopping Enthusiasts" and "Home & Garden" site-wide interest categories, though not segment-confirmed.
- **Data sources:** Internal persona doc; content preference data (roundup pages); temporal data (weekend research, Sun/Mon highest sessions).

## The Value-Conscious Comparison Shopper
**Sample voice:** "Is this actually the best price, or is there a coupon I'm missing? I've got three tabs open comparing this to two other options."

- **Traffic share:** ~20% of traffic; <$1000 is the dominant quiz budget tier
- **Behavior:** Price-first, reads multiple reviews before deciding, actively looks for coupons/deals. Lower AOV ($700-900), gravitates to value brands (Nectar, Brooklyn Bedding).
- **Motivations:** Get a good mattress without overpaying — wants to feel like they found the best deal, not just a cheap product.
- **Trusts:** Transparent pricing, visible deal/coupon callouts, direct price comparisons.
- **Distrusts:** Pages that bury the price or lead with premium options before showing value alternatives.
- **Demographics:** Light inference only: may skew more Chrome/Android by elimination, since Safari usage is tied to the more affluent premium-buyer segment — not directly confirmed, treat as a soft assumption.
- **Data sources:** 3QQ Budget <$1000 (6.1K, dominant tier); internal persona doc; browser/tech data (Safari-affluence insight, used only as inverse signal).

## The Decision-Ready Premium Buyer
**Sample voice:** "I already know I want a Saatva. Just let me get through checkout without ten extra steps."

- **Traffic share:** ~15% of traffic ("Money Bucket" pages), outsized revenue share
- **Behavior:** Arrives via paid search or branded organic already knowing what they want. Fast path to purchase, gravitates to premium brands (Saatva, Helix). Highest AOV ($1,400+).
- **Motivations:** Confirm their choice is right and complete the purchase with minimal friction — not there to be persuaded, there to be validated.
- **Trusts:** Clear top-pick logic, premium cues, polished presentation.
- **Distrusts:** Friction — extra steps, unclear CTAs, anything that slows down a decision they've basically already made.
- **Demographics:** Skews Safari/iOS. The underlying data explicitly ties Safari's 49.2% share of transactions to an affluent, design-conscious demographic — this segment's premium-brand, high-AOV behavior lines up directly with that signal.
- **Data sources:** Internal persona doc; paid campaign data ("Best Mattress - Desktop" = highest revenue campaign).

## The Late-Night Impulse Shopper
**Sample voice:** "It's midnight, I can't sleep again, and I just want something that's going to fix this. I don't want to read a 2,000-word guide right now."

- **Traffic share:** ~10% of traffic (late night 10pm-2am, midnight transaction spike)
- **Behavior:** Shopping at 10pm-2am, likely triggered by an acute bad-sleep-night moment. Emotional, urgency-driven rather than methodical. Decision speed is faster and more reactive than other segments.
- **Motivations:** Fix the immediate frustration of a bad night's sleep, right now — not researching for the future, reacting to tonight.
- **Trusts:** Reassurance and quick resolution — clear "get this now" paths.
- **Distrusts:** Long-form content or multi-step comparison funnels that delay a decision when they're already tired and want to act.
- **Demographics:** General — no segment-specific demographic signal in the data beyond the temporal pattern itself.
- **Data sources:** Temporal data (midnight spike of 2,134 transactions vs. 193-240 during 4am-6am low); internal persona doc.
