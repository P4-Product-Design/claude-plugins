# Swolverine Personas

SIMULATED — grounded in Swolverine's own Shopify Analytics exports (site search queries, product/landing-page conversion and sessions, discounts by order and by code, subscription vs. one-time sales, items bought together, new-vs-returning customers, returns by reason, email subscription status, device/referrer/location sessions, weekly sales timing) and Swolverine's GA4 property exports (acquisition/channel and manual-source data, on-site page-title view counts, engagement/event counts) covering roughly 2025-05-01 through 2026-09-01 depending on export (not the final word on real users). No fixed persona count was requested for this category — this set (8) reflects what the data actually supports, not a template size. Use only to simulate independent reactions in a session — do not let these override or interpret real user research if it exists.

**Demographic caveat:** the underlying exports contain no age/gender/income fields. Any demographic notes below are soft inferences from product mix, search vocabulary, and brand voice (e.g. "Swolverine," "Wolverine Stack," "F Around Find Out" collection), not confirmed segment data — treat them accordingly.

**Data-quality caveats:** (1) the Weekly Sales Patterns export shows hour-0 (midnight) accounting for ~25% of all net sales in the trailing year — 6-9x any other single hour, identically every day of the week, while day-of-week totals are flat (~79K-93K, no real variance). That shape reads as a reporting/timezone-bucketing artifact rather than genuine midnight shopping behavior, so it was not used to build a persona. Excluding hour 0, real intraday sales peak 9am-1pm with a secondary peak around 5-6pm — folded into the two personas below where it's most relevant. (2) The GA4 User Attributes export (Aug 4-31 window) shows Singapore as the 2nd-largest country by active users (42,637) and "Lanzhou," China as the 2nd-largest city sitewide (10,178) despite China's total country figure being only 12,693 — i.e. ~80% of China's GA4-recorded traffic concentrates in one mid-tier city. That concentration pattern is inconsistent with Shopify's own longer-window session-by-location data (where Singapore is a minor share) and reads as bot/proxy traffic rather than real users, so it was not used for geographic or demographic inference in any persona below.

## The Compound-Curious Searcher
**Sample voice:** "I just typed 'anavar' into the search bar because that's what my buddy at the gym is running. I don't actually expect a supplement site to sell that... but if there's something that gets me close, I want to see it."

- **Signal strength:** Anabolic-steroid and SARM terms dominate on-site search volume — "anavar" (1,271), "deca" (613), "masteron" (536), "npp" (508), "winstrol" (432), "tren" (358), "anadrol" (348), "equipoise" (287), "dianabol" (239), "nandrolone" (251), "testosterone" (412) — a volume comparable to or exceeding searches for Swolverine's actual bestselling products. This is backed by a large, dedicated blog content library, not just search dead-ends: full cycle/dosage guides exist for essentially every compound searched, several pulling more views in a single 4-week GA4 window than many real product pages — "Anavar and Testosterone Stack: Cycle Guide" (6,105 views), "Primobolan (Primo) for Beginners" (5,833), "Trenbolone Enanthate" guide (5,413), "Steroid Stack Guide: How to Build a Safe and Effective Cycle" (4,658), "Cycling Dianabol (DBOL)" (4,087), plus a SARM-specific cluster ("RAD-140 (Testolone) vs LGD-4033 (Ligandrol)," "S23 for Bodybuilding"). GA4 referrer data also shows real, direct traffic from bodybuilding/steroid community sites (excelmale.com, steroidsourcetalk.com, tmuscle.co.uk, generationiron.com, stack3d.com) and a small but real AI-chatbot referral trickle (chatgpt.com, gemini.google.com, perplexity.ai) — consistent with someone asking an AI assistant about a compound and clicking through a citation.
- **Behavior:** Lands on-site already primed with a specific compound name in mind, usually from gym or forum talk, often via Google or a direct link from a bodybuilding forum. A large share land directly on one of Swolverine's own cycle-guide articles and read it in real depth (not just a quick search dead-end) before either bouncing or following an internal link toward an adjacent legal category (post-cycle-therapy collection, testosterone booster, estrogen blocker) — the site's own PCT and "Best Steroids to Stack with X"-style content is written to bridge exactly that handoff. This isn't a fringe path into the site — "FINDOUT," tied to the "F Around Find Out" collection this persona often lands in, is the single most-redeemed discount code sitewide (614 orders, $40,184 gross), well ahead of any seasonal or general promo code.
- **Motivations:** Find something that replicates or supports the effect of a compound they've heard about — curiosity or an active cycle, not brand loyalty or research-mode browsing.
- **Trusts:** Direct, unembarrassed acknowledgment of what they're actually looking for (e.g. clear "legal alternative" or "cycle support" framing) over vague wellness language.
- **Distrusts:** Generic "boost your gains" copy that doesn't engage with what they specifically searched for. Feels like a bait-and-switch if the search just returns nothing relevant.
- **Demographics:** Soft inference only — search vocabulary (steroid slang, "buddy at the gym" framing) skews toward male, bodybuilding/strength-training-adjacent users; not confirmed by any demographic field in the data.
- **Data sources:** Searches by search query (2026-05-04–2026-09-01); Conversion Rate – Weekly (post-cycle-therapy collection rows); Sales by discount codes (2025-05-01–2026-08-31); GA4 Engagement overview (page title/views, 2026-08-04–2026-08-31); GA4 Acquisition overview (session manual source, same window).

## The Peptide & Longevity Researcher
**Sample voice:** "I found this because I googled 'MOTS-c dosage,' not because I was shopping. I just want the actual protocol — how much, how often, what to watch for. I'm not really thinking about buying anything right now."

- **Signal strength:** This is the single largest entry point onto the entire site. Swolverine has published dedicated dosage/protocol blog content directly targeting peptide and longevity compounds, and it dominates traffic: "MOTS-c for Beginners" is the #1 landing page sitewide (137,058 sessions — ahead of the homepage's 87,783), followed by "Ipamorelin Dosage Guide" (71,875 sessions), "BPC-157 Dosage Guide" (45,768), and "Tesamorelin for Bodybuilders" (35,120). The content library extends far beyond these top few — GA4 page-view data (a single 4-week window) shows dozens more at real volume: "IGF-1 LR3 for Beginners," "CJC-1295 For Beginners," "SS-31 Peptide," "Selank," "AOD-9604 Dosage Guide," "Epitalon," "GHK-Cu Peptide for Hair Growth," "TB-500 Dosage Guide," and multiple Retatrutide-specific articles, each in the 400-2,200 view range in that window alone. This lines up with the matching search-term cluster — "nad"/"nad+" (2,543 combined), "peptides" (680), "bpc"/"bpc-157" (1,108 combined), "ghk-cu"/"ghk" (839 combined), "retatrutide"/"reta" (778 combined), "mots-c" (683 combined), "ipamorelin" (280), "semax" (259), "ss-31" (254), "cjc" (245), "glutathione" (396), "kpv" (388), plus blend names "klow" (798) and "glow" (305). GA4 referrer data also shows direct traffic from peptide-specific community/reference sites (peptidedosages.com, bpc157.io, compoundsolutions.com, licensedpeptides.com, community.peptidecritic.com) and a small AI-chatbot referral trickle (chatgpt.com, gemini.google.com, perplexity.ai).
- **Behavior:** Arrives directly on one of these articles (usually via organic search for the exact compound, sometimes via a peptide-community site or an AI chatbot citation), reads for the dosage/protocol answer, and leaves without touching the store. Commercial intent on these pages is close to zero relative to their traffic — the MOTS-c article converts only 48 cart-adds and 14 checkouts from 137K sessions; Ipamorelin only 20/5 from 71.9K; BPC-157 only 9/1 from 45.8K. This is the largest single contributor to the site's high, rising bounce rate (83–89%).
- **Motivations:** Proactive healthspan/recovery/injury-recovery research — arrives with a specific protocol question (dosage, stacking, side effects), not a purchase need. Distinct from the Compound-Curious Searcher's bodybuilding-cycle framing even though both search for things Swolverine doesn't sell.
- **Trusts:** That the content itself already ranks and reads as credible (it's driving more traffic than almost anything else on the site) — trusts substantive, specific protocol information over being sold to.
- **Distrusts:** An article that turns out to be thin bait for an unrelated protein/creatine upsell, or that doesn't actually answer the dosage question that brought them there.
- **Demographics:** No data available — this is a content/research-vocabulary signal, not a demographic one.
- **Data sources:** Sessions by landing page (2026-05-04–2026-09-01); Searches by search query (2026-05-04–2026-09-01); Bounce rate over time (2026-05-04–2026-09-01, rising trend); GA4 Engagement overview (page title/views, 2026-08-04–2026-08-31); GA4 Acquisition overview (session manual source, same window).

## The Cycle-Support Buyer
**Sample voice:** "I know exactly what I'm here for — ZMT to keep my test up, DHEA, and something for estrogen. This isn't random browsing, I've done this before."

- **Signal strength:** Testosterone Booster ZMT, DHEA, and Equilone (natural estrogen blocker) all show real, converting traffic together — ZMT: 7,669 sessions / 1.34% conversion / 103 completed checkouts; DHEA: 4,036 sessions / 0.45% conversion / 18 checkouts; Equilone: 6,041 sessions / 0.31% conversion / 19 checkouts. ZMT itself is a named search term ("zmt", 327 searches), as is the brand's flagship stack ("wolverine", 392; "wolverine stack", 321). Items bought together confirms this is a real, deliberate combination rather than incidental overlap: ZMT + DHEA is purchased together 31 times ($87.39 AOV), and a three-item Krill Oil + ZMT + DHEA stack appears 23 times ($110.27 AOV). The "SP-POSTRECOVERY" discount code (21 redemptions) is a further named, dedicated path for this exact behavior. GA4 content data shows a direct editorial bridge into these products: articles like "Estrogen Blockers vs Aromatase Inhibitors: What Every Athlete Needs to Know," "Best PCT Supplements for Different Steroid Cycles," and "How Long to Wait Before Starting PCT" sit alongside dozens of steroid cycle guides, giving the Compound-Curious Searcher's content an explicit on-ramp toward Equilone/ZMT/DHEA.
- **Behavior:** Unlike the two search/research personas above, this persona actually converts — they know the specific SKU names going in, often navigating to a "post-cycle-therapy" or "f-around-find-out" collection rather than arriving cold. The ZMT + DHEA combo skews heavily toward first-time customers (24 of 31 orders), suggesting most people arrive already intending to buy the full stack rather than building it up gradually over repeat visits. Discounts by order shows ZMT and DHEA both among the most frequently discounted line items (220 and 76 orders respectively), so this purchase is usually happening at a promotional price, not full price.
- **Motivations:** Manage hormone levels — either genuine post-cycle recovery or general natural testosterone support — with product names they already trust from prior use or gym-community word of mouth.
- **Trusts:** Named, specific formulations (ZMT, Equilone) with a track record; a discount code being present and working.
- **Distrusts:** Being asked to re-research or reconsider a decision they've already made; friction at checkout when they already know what they want.
- **Demographics:** Soft inference — likely returning/repeat customers given the product category and discount-code reliance; the "F Around Find Out" and "Wolverine" branding suggests a male, bodybuilding-culture audience, but this is inferred from copy/branding, not a data field.
- **Data sources:** Conversion Rate by product (2026-01-01–2026-09-01); Discounts by order (2026-01-01–2026-09-01); Searches by search query; Conversion Rate – Weekly (post-cycle-therapy, f-around-find-out collection rows); Items bought together (2026-03-01–2026-09-01); Sales by discount codes (2025-05-01–2026-08-31); GA4 Engagement overview (page title/views, 2026-08-04–2026-08-31).

## The Creatine-First Foundational Shopper
**Sample voice:** "I don't need anything fancy — just a plain creatine monohydrate that's actually what it says on the label. This is the one supplement I know works, so I'm not going to overthink it."

- **Signal strength:** Creatine Monohydrate is the single largest traffic driver on the site by a wide margin (129,749 sessions, 113,778 unique visitors) and also the best-relatively-converting major product (1.17% conversion over the full period, 2,815 checkout completions) — outperforming every other product on both volume and rate. It's also the single most-discounted line item (599 orders). Items bought together confirms creatine's anchor role directly — it's the common ingredient in the site's two largest bundle combinations: Multivitamin + Creatine Monohydrate (121 orders, heavily returning-customer at 108 of 121) and Whey Protein Isolate + Creatine Monohydrate (116 orders, more evenly split at 76 first-time / 40 returning).
- **Behavior:** Arrives via search or direct, goes straight to the creatine product page rather than browsing, and converts at a meaningfully higher rate than any other product on the site. Price- and discount-aware (99%+ of these orders carry a code) but not deterred by it — this reads as a confident, low-friction, staple restock or first-supplement purchase rather than heavy deliberation. Real (non-artifact) purchase volume peaks 9am-1pm, consistent with a quick, decided errand fit into a workday rather than leisurely evening browsing.
- **Motivations:** Buy the one supplement category with broad, uncontroversial credibility — not chasing a trend, just restocking or starting with the safest, best-evidenced option.
- **Trusts:** Plain-label honesty, a fair/available discount, third-party or scientific credibility for creatine specifically.
- **Distrusts:** Overcomplication — proprietary blends, unnecessary upsells, or pages that bury the plain product behind marketing.
- **Demographics:** General — no segment-specific demographic signal in the data; likely the widest-reach, least-niche persona in this set given the sheer traffic volume.
- **Data sources:** Conversion Rate by product (2026-01-01–2026-09-01); Discounts by order (2026-01-01–2026-09-01); Overall Product Performance – Overtime; Weekly sales patterns (2025-09-01–2026-09-01, hour-0 excluded as artifact); Items bought together (2026-03-01–2026-09-01).

## The Protein Comparison Shopper
**Sample voice:** "Everyone's whey protein claims to be the best. I'm here to check ingredients and price per serving, but honestly I've got two other tabs open too."

- **Signal strength:** Whey Protein Isolate is the second-largest traffic driver on the site (95,078 sessions, 86,790 unique visitors) but converts at less than a third the rate of Creatine Monohydrate (0.38% vs 1.17%) — the widest traffic-to-conversion gap of any major product on the site. Swolverine itself runs a dedicated "Performance Max | Whey" Google Ads campaign (2,291 sessions), confirming this is a category the brand knows it has to actively compete for rather than one that sells itself.
- **Behavior:** Lands on the whey protein page in real volume but leaves at a far higher rate than the traffic alone would predict, consistent with active comparison-shopping against bigger, more established protein brands rather than immediate purchase intent. Weak, low-sample supporting signal (only 18 returned units sitewide across the full year, so treat cautiously): Whey Protein Isolate has the most "changed-my-mind" and "dietary-requirements" returns of any product (2 and 1 respectively) — consistent with a segment that keeps comparing even after purchase.
- **Motivations:** Find the best combination of ingredient quality, flavor, and price-per-serving in a crowded category — not brand-loyal yet, still deciding.
- **Trusts:** Transparent ingredient panels, clear price-per-serving math, real differentiation from category leaders.
- **Distrusts:** A product page that doesn't answer "why this over [bigger competitor brand]" directly.
- **Demographics:** General — no segment-specific demographic signal in the data.
- **Data sources:** Conversion Rate by product (2026-01-01–2026-09-01); Top returned products with reasons (2025-09-01–2026-09-01, small sample — 18 total returned units sitewide); GA4 Acquisition overview (Google Ads campaign sessions, 2026-08-04–2026-08-31).

## The Set-and-Forget Subscriber
**Sample voice:** "I honestly don't even think about this order anymore, it just shows up. As long as the price doesn't creep up on me without warning, I'm not going anywhere."

- **Signal strength:** Subscription orders make up nearly half of all orders in the trailing 15 months (7,050 subscription vs 7,095 one-time — a near-even split) but subscription line items carry almost no discount (-$2,971 against $309,475 gross, well under 1%) — sharply different from one-time orders, which are discounted at roughly 17.5% of gross (-$91,652 against $521,804). Subscription order count also grew 5.5% year over year even as one-time order count fell 1.2%.
- **Behavior:** Already committed to an auto-ship plan and doesn't seek or need a coupon each cycle the way a one-time buyer does — the subscription price is effectively the discount already baked in, so there's no active deal-hunting behavior at all once enrolled. This is a growing share of the business relative to manual one-time purchasing, which is shrinking.
- **Motivations:** Convenience and price certainty on a known-necessary staple — actively wants to stop thinking about the reorder decision, not to hunt for the best deal every cycle.
- **Trusts:** Stable, predictable pricing; reliable delivery timing; an easy way to skip, pause, or modify a shipment without a fight.
- **Distrusts:** A subscription price that quietly increases, or a cancellation/pause flow with unnecessary friction.
- **Demographics:** General — no segment-specific demographic signal in the data.
- **Data sources:** Subscription vs one-time sales (2025-05-01–2026-08-31).

## The Manual Reorder Deal-Seeker
**Sample voice:** "I'm not on a subscription — I like picking my own moment, and I always check if there's a code first. There's always a code."

- **Signal strength:** One-time orders are discounted far more heavily than subscription orders (~17.5% of gross vs under 1%), and code redemptions are dominated by broad promotional codes rather than personalized ones — "FINDOUT" (614 orders, $40,184 gross), "50ONFRIDAY2025" (242 orders, $38,850), "SUMMERSHRED" (148 orders, $12,649), "BUY MORE, SAVE MORE" (86 orders), and "CYBERMONDAY25" (33 orders) are among the top codes redeemed. Orders by customer email subscription status (trailing 120 days) shows email-subscribed customers drove 81% of all orders (3,937 of 4,832) — this segment's reorder behavior is very likely email-triggered (a campaign or code landing in their inbox) rather than an unprompted return visit. Returning customers make up an increasing share of monthly customers across the data — from the mid-30s%/high-50s% in Jan-Apr (35.6-57.5%) up to 55-76% May-Sep (e.g. 69.7% in June, 68.3% in July, 63.2% in August) — a genuine upward trend, not a flat baseline.
- **Behavior:** Deliberately returns to buy again but chooses to do so manually rather than subscribe, typically prompted by an email/marketing touch rather than organic recall, then actively checking for whatever sitewide or seasonal code is currently running before checking out. A meaningful share of "return" purchases are also Store Credit-driven (122 orders) — a return/replacement-triggered reorder rather than fresh purchase intent. Returning-customer AOV runs slightly below new-customer AOV in most months (e.g. $48.17 vs $62.75 in June, $44.56 vs $57.83 in July), consistent with restocking familiar, lower-cost SKUs. Real (non-artifact) reorder volume clusters 9am-1pm and again around 5-6pm, fitting into a routine (morning errand or post-work wind-down) rather than a planned shopping session.
- **Motivations:** Maintain an existing routine while still feeling like they got a deal each time — the code-hunting itself is part of the expected experience, not a special win — while keeping the active control over timing that a subscription would remove.
- **Trusts:** A visibly-promoted, working code at checkout (FINDOUT, seasonal sale codes) — especially one that matches what arrived in their email; a fast, familiar checkout.
- **Distrusts:** Being asked to pay full price when they know a sitewide or seasonal promo is usually running; any change to a formula, price, or checkout flow that disrupts an established routine.
- **Demographics:** General — no segment-specific demographic signal in the data.
- **Data sources:** Returning customer rate over time (2026-05-04–2026-09-01, and prior-period comparison back to 2026-01-01); Average order value over time – New vs. Returning; Discounts by order (2026-01-01–2026-09-01); Sales by discount codes (2025-05-01–2026-08-31); Subscription vs one-time sales (2025-05-01–2026-08-31); Weekly sales patterns (2025-09-01–2026-09-01, hour-0 excluded as artifact); Orders by customer email subscription status (trailing 120 days).

## The Editorial-Referred Researcher
**Sample voice:** "I read about this on one of those 'best supplements' roundup articles, not an ad. That's actually why I clicked through — someone else already vetted it."

- **Signal strength:** A meaningful, non-social share of sessions arrive via third-party editorial/affiliate content rather than paid ads or direct search — "fortune" (63,254 sessions), "forbes" (6,121), "garagegymreviews" (2,114) — collectively larger than all social referrers combined (instagram 23,216 + facebook 22,883 + youtube 9,911 = 56,010). GA4's more granular manual-source data shows the actual referring domain is "shop.fortune.com" (11,166 sessions) specifically, alongside "fortune.com" (236) — most of this traffic comes through a shoppable commerce page bearing Fortune's branding rather than a plain editorial article, which likely carries even more pre-formed purchase intent than a standard "best of" roundup.
- **Behavior:** Arrives having already read a comparison, "best of," or shoppable recommendation page elsewhere, so shows up with some pre-formed trust and context rather than cold. Likely overlaps with the Creatine-First or Protein Comparison personas in terms of which product page they land on, but is distinguished by the referral path and the trust it carries in.
- **Motivations:** Make a confident decision informed by a third party they already trust, rather than evaluating the brand's own claims from scratch.
- **Trusts:** The referring publication's credibility; a landing page that honors what the article said rather than contradicting or overselling past it.
- **Distrusts:** A landing experience that feels disconnected from what the article promised (different product, different price, different framing).
- **Demographics:** General — no segment-specific demographic signal in the data; Fortune and Forbes audiences are typically broader/more mainstream than GarageGymReviews' more niche fitness-enthusiast base, but this is inferred from the publications themselves, not from Swolverine's own data.
- **Data sources:** Sessions by referrer (2026-05-04–2026-09-01); GA4 Acquisition overview (session manual source, 2026-08-04–2026-08-31).
